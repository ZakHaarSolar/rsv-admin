**What's the best thing you've built, discovered, or pulled off?**

Red Solar Viva: a studio of one that ships like a team of thirty. I run it with my co-creator Aqua'Riia, and most of it was built in the last six months with Claude Code.

• Escáner Vibracional: a bilingual consciousness app, live on iOS, Android, Mac and the web. First line of code to the App Store in about two months. It measures six areas of your life and turns them into a 0 to 100 Light Index. Its heart is Espejo (Mirror): a voice companion with long-term memory that speaks its own language of consciousness, drawn from a 4,700-passage library that includes the nine books I wrote. It looks at the photos you send, remembers you across conversations and answers out loud. Two more AI tools decode food labels and dreams.
• Terra Cristal: a pixel-art tactical RPG in the spirit of Shining Force, where you win battles by freeing your enemies. Chapter 1 is free in the browser; the paid version went live this week.
• Three more games (two already playable), five animated films and four albums.

Under the hood: more than 550,000 lines of TypeScript that I specified, directed and tested, 61 serverless functions, 355 database migrations, offline sync and payments. Most people assume this takes a team and funding. I don't type the code; I direct it, the way a film director directs a crew.

**What do you believe that almost nobody else does?**

That we design our lives before we're born, and then forget on purpose.

The family, the country, the obstacles: chosen. The amnesia isn't a bug; it's the gameplay feature that makes the choices real. Take that seriously and victimhood stops being an option, because every constraint becomes a brief you wrote for yourself.

For me this isn't a comforting idea, it's an operating system. It's why I didn't wait for a team, funding or permission to build an app company, a game studio and a film studio at once. It's also why I build games: a good game is the closest model we have of incarnation. You accept limits so that growth can mean something. Terra Cristal is that idea, playable.

**What are you obsessed with right now that has nothing to do with any of the above?**

Whether attention can move matter.

Every day I sit with a square of aluminum foil balanced on a pin under a glass jar. After months of practice, it spins when I focus on it, every time I sit down. I know the obvious explanation: body heat warms the glass and the air currents push the foil. So I'm not asking anyone to believe me. I'm building the test that would kill it: the foil inside a vacuum chamber (no air, no currents), a thermal camera on the setup, a hand warmer at body temperature as the control, and focus windows chosen by a random number generator and logged before I look. If it still spins, I publish the raw video and the protocol. If it doesn't, I publish that too.

I've lived with the same kind of question for years with blindfolded perception, which I taught for six years. Same rule there: test it until it breaks or it doesn't.

**What are you doing now, and what's next?**

Now: I work 40 hours a week evaluating multimodal AI training data (long multi-camera robotics video), and I build Red Solar Viva at night. This week Terra Cristal opened its paid version. Escáner is live on iOS, Android, Mac and the web. PRISMA TD, a tower defense built around mixing beams of light, is in development.

Next, and what $50,000 changes: it buys me a full year on this instead of nights. In order: (1) Terra Cristal on Steam, in English, with a demo for Next Fest; (2) Escáner English-first, so Espejo can speak to the world and not only to Spanish speakers; (3) the vacuum-chamber experiment, run in public with the protocol posted first. The long game: a studio of worlds, tools and stories that leave people more awake than they came in.
